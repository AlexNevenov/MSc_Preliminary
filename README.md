# Asymmetric Model of Galactic Fountains for the Origins of Milky Way's Intermediate- and High-Velocity Clouds
*Preliminary results of my MSc thesis (University of Groningen, with Prof. F. Fraternali and F. J. Urbina Jiménez).*

My thesis asks *'Why are intermediate- and high-velocity clouds (IVCs and HVCs) asymmetric between the northern and southern hemispheres of the Milky Way?'* To answer it, I run dynamical galactic-fountain simulations and produce Sun-centered (l, b, v) datacubes. I then compare, contrast, and fit these to observed all-sky HI datacubes. So far, I have done this extensively for the Leiden/Argentine/Bonn (LAB) survey ([Kalberla et al. 2005](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0504140)), so that we can make comparisons with earlier work such as [Marasco et al. (2012; hence *"Marasco+12"*)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.419.1107M). The model used so far is mirror-symmetric (the northern cube is mirrored into the south) and serves as the baseline for the asymmetric treatment that comes next.

This work is in progress and not peer reviewed yet. This repository contains notebooks for selected parts of the work (last updated on 26 October 2026), so that you can see what I have done so far, my workflow, and the conclusions I have drawn.

## Current state
- The preference for condensation-driven accretion is recovered robustly, as in Marasco+12.
- Finer distinctions (e.g., neutral vs. ionized fountains, and the size of the differences) depend on how the model–data residual is defined. See Notebooks 1 and 3.
- Several fitted optima sit on the edge of the parameter box, so Notebook 5 includes three checks on how far they can be trusted.
- Next: I am currently implementing separate hemisphere treatment and model comparison with Bayesian tools. This is work in progress and not yet in the repository.

| [`5_grid_search_4d`](Notebooks/5_grid_search_4d.ipynb)           | A four-parameter grid of 512 models (`hv0`, `alpha_conds`, `alpha_hv`, `f_ion`), fitted with a Universal Kriging Gaussian Process on nine residual definitions. Section 6 adds three checks: the mirror-symmetric model fitted to each hemisphere alone, a mock sky with a known answer, and Marasco+12's best-fit point in our parameter surface. | An interior optimum is recovered in the mock-sky test, but the squared and relative residuals are pulled toward the box edges more than the absolute one, and several optima of the real fit sit on an edge. Fitted to each hemisphere alone, the absolute and relative residuals give different optima, while the squared one does not. Marasco+12's best-fit point is a few per cent to ~12% above our optimum in residual for most absolute and relative residuals (29% for one), and indistinguishable for the squared one. This is not a full Bayesian comparison, so the results are approximate but informative. |
| [`6_grid_search_4d_ahv0`](Notebooks/6_grid_search_4d_ahv0.ipynb) | Same as Notebook 5, but with `alpha_hv` fixed to 0 (a constant ejection velocity, as in Marasco+12) and `vlag` free.                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | For two of the three Marasco-set residuals, the optima land closer to Marasco+12's parameters in `hv0` and `alpha_conds` (about 60–63 km/s and 5–6 Gyr⁻¹, versus their 70 km/s and 6.3 Gyr⁻¹).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| [`7_best_fit_model`](Notebooks/7_best_fit_model.ipynb)           | Plots for our two best-fit models next to the three Marasco+12 models (their parameters, but generated with our code) against LAB, as l–v and channel maps with σ contours.                                                                                                                                                                        | By eye, our best fits match LAB about as well as the Marasco+12 models and, in some places, slightly better.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |

*Parameters:* `hv0` is the characteristic ejection (or 'kick') velocity (km/s), `alpha_conds` is the condensation rate (Gyr⁻¹, 0 = none), `alpha_hv` is the radial dependence of the ejection velocity (0 = constant), `f_ion` the ionization setting (0 = neutral fountain, 1 = ionized on the way up and neutral on the way down), and `vlag` is the lag (in km/s) of the corona relative to the local circular speed.
